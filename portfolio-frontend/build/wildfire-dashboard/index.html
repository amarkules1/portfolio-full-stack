Canada Spotlight

2023: Canadian Pyro-Crisis

The Global Record Breaker

17.6M Hectares Burned

By far the most destructive wildfire season in North American history, burning more than triple the long-term Canadian average. Extreme drought and early summer heat caused concurrent outbreaks across Quebec, Alberta, BC, and the NWT. Standard firefighting resources were overwhelmed, leading to the mobilization of thousands of international firefighters. Smoke plumes traveled thousands of miles, choking air quality in New York City, Chicago, and Western Europe, and releasing over 1.7 billion tonnes of CO₂ equivalent.

Data Authorities & Citations

Wildfire reporting is subject to updates as post-season satellite burn mapping (like NBAC) corrects tactical field reports. Consistent digital baseline databases for both the US and Canada begin in 1983; data compiled prior to this period relies on fragmented state/provincial paper systems and is not considered comparable.

National Interagency Fire Center (NIFC) Coordinates wildland firefighting agencies in the US. Author of the NICC Wildland Fire Summary and Statistics (Redbooks) which host historical state-by-state data. NIFC Official Statistics
Canadian Wildland Fire Information System (CWFIS) Managed by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan). Compiles the Canadian National Fire Database (CNFDB), standardizing fire points and perimeters from 1980-present. CWFIS Data Portal
Canadian Interagency Forest Fire Centre (CIFFC) Coordinates resource sharing across Canada's provinces and territories, compiling daily situation summaries and final annual reports. CIFFC Annual Reports
